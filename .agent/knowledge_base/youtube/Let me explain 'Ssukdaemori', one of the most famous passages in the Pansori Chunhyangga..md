## 📖 Core Content
Let me explain **'Ssukdaemori'**, one of the most famous passages in the Pansori *Chunhyangga*.

1. Overview

'Ssukdaemori' depicts Chunhyang lamenting her wretched plight while imprisoned for refusing Byeon Hak-do's advances, while longing for her beloved (Lee Mong-ryong). It is considered the highlight of the 'Okjungga' (Songs of Prison) in *Chunhyangga*.

2. Meaning of the Title

'Ssukdaemori' means 'hair disheveled like a field of weeds.' It is a title that symbolically portrays Chunhyang's appearance, which has become a mess because she has been unable to comb her hair for a long time while suffering hardships in prison.

3. Musical Characteristics

Rhythm: It is primarily sung in the Jinyangjo rhythm. Jinyangjo is the slowest rhythm in Pansori, making it suitable for expressing Chunhyang's deep sorrow, despair, and earnest longing.

Mode (Path): The Gyemyeonjo mode, which conveys a sorrowful feeling, predominates.

4. Main Lyrics

The lyrics begin with a description of Chunhyang's appearance and continue with her resentment and longing for Yi Mong-ryong, as well as her desperate desire to meet him even in death.

"With disheveled hair and a ghostly appearance, in the cold, desolate prison cell, I think only of my beloved..."

(With hair like a mess and a ghostly appearance, on the cold floor of a desolate prison, I think only of my beloved...)

5. Famous Singers

This passage gained immense popularity during the Japanese colonial period when it was sung by the renowned singer Im Bang-ul. At the time, gramophone records featuring her voice sold tens of thousands of copies, sparking a "Ssukdaemori craze." Even today, it remains one of the most familiar songs to the general public who are unfamiliar with Korean traditional music.

Recently, the song is being performed not only as traditional Pansori but also arranged into various genres such as Gayageum Byeongchang, Fusion Korean Music, and Crossover.
