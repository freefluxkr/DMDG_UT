# [[The assassination of Empress Myeongseong .The Tragedy of Okhoru in Gyeongbokgung Palace]]

## 📌 Brief Summary
The Assassination of Empress Myeongseong (Eulmi Incident) was a state-sponsored terrorist incident that occurred at dawn on October 8, 1895 (August 20 in the lunar calendar), in which Japanese soldiers and ronin, led by Japanese Minister Miura Goro and others, illegally stormed Geoncheonggung Palace in Gyeongbokgung Palace and brutally murdered Empress Myeongseong, the mother of the nation of Joseon.

## 📖 Core Content
The Assassination of Empress Myeongseong (Eulmi Incident) was a state-sponsored terrorist incident that occurred at dawn on October 8, 1895 (August 20 in the lunar calendar), in which Japanese soldiers and ronin, led by Japanese Minister Miura Goro and others, illegally stormed Geoncheonggung Palace in Gyeongbokgung Palace and brutally murdered Empress Myeongseong, the mother of the nation of Joseon.

1. Background of the Incident: Pro-Russian Policy and Japan's Sense of Crisis
At the time, Joseon was in a period where Japanese interference in internal affairs was intensifying following the Sino-Japanese War.

However, when Japan yielded to the Triple Intervention (Russia, France, and Germany) and returned the Liaodong Peninsula to the Qing Dynasty, King Gojong and Empress Myeongseong actively pursued a pro-Russian policy (In-a-geo-il, 引俄拒日) to use Russia's power to keep Japan in check. In addition, when Empress Myeongseong attempted to disband the Joseon Army Training Corps trained by Japanese instructors, the Japanese Empire, fearing that its standing in Joseon would be significantly diminished, decided to eliminate her.

2. Development of the Incident: Operation 'Fox Hunt'
This assassination plan, codenamed 'Fox Hunt', was originally scheduled for October 10, but when signs appeared that the disbandment of the Training Corps would be moved up, it was abruptly moved forward and executed at dawn on October 8.

Under the command of Miura Goro, the Japanese Legation Guard, Japanese ronin (masterless samurai) affiliated with the Hanseong Sinbo, and some Joseon Army Training Corps members recruited by them intruded through Gwanghwamun Gate of Gyeongbokgung Palace.

The Japanese rioters brutally killed loyal subjects who stood in their way, such as the Palace Guard Commander Hong Gye-hun and the Minister of the Royal Household Yi Gyeong-jik, and stormed into Okhoru, the residence of Empress Myeongseong. In an attempt to locate Empress Myeongseong, they committed atrocities such as dragging innocent court ladies by their hair, brutally beating them, or throwing them out of windows to their deaths. Eventually, they found Empress Myeongseong and assassinated her with a sword.

To conceal their crime, the mob even went so far as to commit the horrific act of moving Empress Myeongseong's body to a nearby pine forest, dousing it with kerosene, and burning it.

3. Participants in the Crime
This incident involved not only Japanese individuals, including the ringleaders Miura Goro and Okamoto Ryunosuke, but also Korean collaborators.

To disguise this situation as an "internal power struggle within Joseon," Japan forcibly brought Heungseon Daewongun, a political arch-rival of Empress Myeongseong, into the palace and used him as a front for the ostensible pretext. Furthermore, it is known that some Korean military officers, including battalion commanders of the Training Corps such as Woo Beom-seon, Lee Du-hwang, and Lee Jin-ho, as well as pro-Japanese radical reformist figures (such as Yu Gil-jun), collaborated with or aided the incident.

4. Aftermath and Repercussions of the Incident
Immediately after the incident, Japan attempted to frame it as a coup d'état by Heungseon Daewongun and the Korean Training Corps; however, clear eyewitness testimonies from foreigners present at the scene (such as Russian architect Sabatin and American instructor Dye) exposed Japan's atrocities in detail to the international community.

As international condemnation intensified, Japan imprisoned 48 participants, including Miura, in Hiroshima Prison and pretended to bring them to trial, but ultimately displayed a deceptive attitude by releasing all of them on the grounds of "insufficient evidence." This unprecedented atrocity, in which foreign troops and rioters stormed the royal palace and brutally murdered the Queen Mother, stimulated the extreme anger and anti-Japanese sentiment of the Joseon people, becoming the spark that ignited the Anti-Japanese Righteous Army Movement (Eulmi Righteous Army).
